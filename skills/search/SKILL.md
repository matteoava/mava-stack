# Search

Find the most useful existing material for a need and explain why it fits. Answer with original resources, evidence, and coverage, not a long list of filename matches.

## Uses

Read the applicable instructions below before acting.

- [ORCHESTRATE.MD shared execution rules](../../ORCHESTRATE.MD#shared-execution-rules).
- The working profile and local AGENTS.md for authorized roots and selected connectors.
- context, prepare-session, or [storyline](../storyline/SKILL.md) when the results feed later work.

## 1. Frame the search

- Establish the need, audience, intended use, artifact types, and breadth. Finding analytics discovery decks is different from proving those sessions happened or finding an approved template.
- A reference in memory or guidance is a lead, not permission to open another initiative. Ask only about scope or meaning that would materially change the search.
- Derive search terms from the topic, business outcome, process, product or capability aliases, session type, customer or initiative identifiers, and relevant dates.
- Start narrow and widen within the authorized scope when results are weak. Never scan unrelated personal folders or switch accounts or providers silently.

## 2. Discover candidates

- Search folder listings, existing indexes, filenames, and document content with the host's supported tools. Match the business purpose as well as the wording.
- Follow pointers from existing context to original resources. Search the selected email or team discussions when they can locate an attachment, a session, an owner, or a better version.
- Treat snippets, metadata, AI summaries, and host recall as leads. Open promising originals with format-aware readers, and inspect the relevant slides, sections, sheets, comments, or notes. Say when content or an attachment cannot be read.
- Stay read-only: no new index or catalog, no indexing backend, no channel joins, no access requests, and no monitoring.
- Record the roots, sources, query variants, date filters, pagination, and access limits actually used. An unavailable location or partial search is a coverage gap, not a negative finding.

## 3. Verify fit and source role

For each promising candidate, establish:

- **Identity:** customer or initiative, original location or stable ID, title, owner where known, date, and version.
- **Evidence:** what was inspected and exactly where, such as slide numbers, a section heading, or a sheet and range. Label metadata-only candidates.
- **Fit:** relevant topics, audience, stage, and scope, and what the resource does not cover. A reusable component may support only part of a customer journey.
- **Role:** approved template or editable base, composition example, customer artifact, draft, conceptual demo, pilot, or production evidence, where supported.
- **Applicability:** product release, environment, calculation, or commercial assumptions to recheck before reuse.

A deck proves that material exists, not that a session happened. To answer "have we run this session?", look for meeting, attendance, recap, or follow-up evidence, and keep confirmed, planned, and unverified activity apart. Missing delivery evidence does not prove it never happened.

When several references serve different purposes, explain each role rather than naming one universal base. Customer-specific facts and commitments stay with their original initiative.

## 4. Resolve versions and rank

- Deduplicate identical files, forwarded attachments, and exports without deleting originals. Group versions by lineage, and prefer the canonical or current human-edited version over whichever filename sorts last. Keep an older version when it is the requested baseline or serves a different purpose.
- Rank by inspected relevance, audience and scope fit, evidence quality, applicability, and reuse effort. Across customers, group results by initiative; duplicate copies do not count as separate examples.
- Flag adaptation needs such as customer data, notes, hidden sheets, embedded objects, stale rates or product claims, incomplete functionality, and environment differences. Do not imply a sanitization or recalculation that was not performed.
- Access is not sharing clearance. State known or unknown sharing limits. Copying, adapting, producing, and sharing are separate work.

## 5. Return a usable resource set

Lead with the answer and the strongest matches, grouped by initiative or resource role as the request needs. For each recommended item, give:

| Field | Include |
| --- | --- |
| Resource | Original clickable location, title, customer or initiative, and version or date. |
| Why it fits | Specific inspected content and useful slide, section, or sheet locations. |
| Evidence status | Content inspected, metadata only, attachment unavailable, and session-delivery evidence when asked. |
| Reuse guidance | What is useful, what needs adapting or verifying, and sharing limits. |

Prefer original shared locations the audience can open over one person's local copy. Never invent links, owners, approval, or version status, and explain pointers teammates may not be able to open.

Close with the actual coverage and material gaps. Say "no match found within the searched scope" when that is true. If fewer examples are supported than requested, return those and the gap rather than padding the list with weak matches.

## Completion

Stop when the requested set is supported, or when progress depends on a stated access or evidence gap. Keep exploration bounded by the task and offer a focused expansion when it would help.

Results stay in chat unless a saved list is requested. Leave originals unchanged, and make no reference copies or memory writes by default. If the results must become a formal document or deck, produce it through storyline.
