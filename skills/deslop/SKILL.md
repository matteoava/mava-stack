# Deslop

Make material clear, credible, and useful to its audience. Remove what gets in the way while keeping the detail needed to understand, decide, or act. A detailed explanation or full set of minutes can be the right outcome.

## Uses

Read the applicable instructions below before acting.

- [ORCHESTRATE.MD shared execution rules](../../ORCHESTRATE.MD#shared-execution-rules).
- verify for doubtful claims, and [storyline](../storyline/SKILL.md) when the material is a deck or formal document.

## 1. Establish the editing brief

- Read the material, its purpose, audience, language, intended decision, and requested depth, using existing context and sources where available. Keep the author's voice, directness, and level of detail rather than imposing a generic tone.
- Identify the protected meaning: facts, numbers and units, names and IDs, scope and exclusions, uncertainty, decision and approval status, owners, commitments, action boundaries, and important technical distinctions.
- Separate quoted source text, reported claims, and the author's interpretation.
- For a file, edit the current human-edited version. Permission to edit does not authorize sending, publishing, or replacing approved baselines. Ask only when an ambiguity would change the meaning or audience.

## 2. Diagnose what gets in the reader's way

Look for the problems this material actually has:

- Filler, promotional language, generic introductions, repeated conclusions, and unsupported superlatives.
- Vague subjects, buried decisions, unexplained terms, long sentences, weak verbs, and needless abstraction.
- Claims stronger than their evidence, invented precision, hidden assumptions, and proposals phrased as commitments.
- Repeated or misplaced detail, inconsistent terms, disconnected steps, and lists or tables that hide relationships.
- Working commentary, debugging history, file paths, or process notes that do not belong in reader-facing text.

Concision must not turn a tentative option into an assured capability, drop an exclusion, or make a complex system misleadingly simple.

## 3. Rewrite with purpose

- Lead with the point the reader needs. Use plain language, concrete subjects and verbs, and connected paragraphs. Use lists for parallel items, tables for comparison, and a walkthrough where sequence matters.
- Keep the requested language unless translation is part of the task. Correct errors without inventing facts, rationale, product claims, figures, owners, or deadlines.
- Keep distinct concepts distinct: component versus complete journey, request versus approved scope, reported progress versus observed outcome.
- Consolidate repetition and remove empty wording while keeping useful examples, qualifications, and decision-relevant detail. Detailed minutes keep every substantive point and its relationships rather than shrinking into a generic summary.
- Keep provenance in the appropriate internal record rather than putting internal filenames or private notes in customer-facing text. Keep audience-appropriate citations and legally or contractually required wording.
- When the real problem is comprehension, apply [principle 7](../../PRINCIPLES.md#7-explain-until-the-user-understands). Editing alone does not show that the user understands or agrees.

## 4. Check meaning and presentation

Compare the revision with the original and the available evidence:

- Did every substantive requirement, decision, exclusion, responsibility, and necessary detail survive?
- Do numbers, units, dates, IDs, attributions, and version references still match?
- Are uncertainty, conditional feasibility, missing evidence, and approval status still visible?
- Is the result easier for this audience to follow, without new unsupported conclusions?

Flag doubtful claims for verify rather than inventing a correction or calling an unverified rewrite fact-checked. Keep exact quotations exact, or turn them into clearly attributed paraphrase.

For a deck or formal document, follow storyline: work on the current artifact or a copy of an inspected base, keep layout and styles, and render the changed pages to check text fit, hierarchy, and inherited notes and metadata. If a new file is needed and no usable base exists, return the content and ask for a reference first. A prose cleanup does not justify redesigning the artifact.

## Output and completion

Return the revised material in the requested format. Keep edit commentary short, and explain only meaning-sensitive changes and open claims. If files changed, name the actual file and version and the checks performed.

Save authorized file changes under the shared rules. Do not create parallel copies, tracking documents, or a changelog just to show the editing. Update memory only within its capture mandate.

The work is complete when the material is clearer, its meaning is preserved, and any uncertainty or validation gap is explicit. Consequential content changes, approval, sending, publication, and implementation remain separate actions with their own authority.
